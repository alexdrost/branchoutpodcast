## Why this episode

Something your work depended on got taken away, and you have quietly treated the goal it served as gone with it. Tom Boniface was handed Toronto and Pittsburgh as new markets midway through the pandemic, never traveled to either, and built both anyway. If you have caught yourself saying that your kind of work only happens face to face, he offers a formula for isolating the part of the situation you still control.

## Guest

Tom Boniface is Vice President of Business Development with Hilco Global, based in Chicago. He was assigned two unfamiliar territories midway through COVID and developed them without ever visiting, leaning on colleagues inside his firm for named introductions and market context. He is a returning guest, and he spends a fair portion of this episode turning the questions back on Alex Drost. Find him at [linkedin.com/in/thomas-j-boniface](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thomas-j-boniface).

## Key ideas

**Change is better described as adjustment, and only one term in the equation moves.** Boniface works from E + R = O: events plus response equals outcomes. The events of 2020 changed and the outcomes he owed himself did not, which leaves the response as the only variable to solve for.

**Believing there is one way to do something is the trap, and it resolves into blame, complain, defend.** He calls that pattern BCD and refuses the premise behind it. His test case is relationship building, where the received wisdom in dealmaking was that you had to be in the room.

**A territory can be built entirely virtually, and he has the case to show for it.** Two markets he did not know, no travel, real traction. He is careful not to claim he did it alone: internal colleagues supplied names and market context, and he told new contacts plainly that he was new and trying to get out there.

**Uncertainty is handled by inventorying what is genuinely controllable, not by planning harder.** Boniface's distinction is between what you actually control and what you merely hope to control. Lean on the first and you manufacture enough certainty to drain the emotion out of the rest.

**Remote work deleted transition time, so it has to be rebuilt deliberately.** A 30-minute morning walk or a meditation stands in for the commute, and giving each room in the house a single function means entering the space sets the focus.

**Avoiding what you are bad at quietly forecloses growth.** Drost admits he does not golf because he is not good at it and names that as the wrong reason. Boniface's counter is to substitute something you actually enjoy and bring people into it, which reaches the same outcome without the driving range.

## Try this

- Take 30 minutes this week on one thing you have written off as out of reach, and write down the exact sentence you keep telling yourself about it. That is Drost's call to action in the episode, and the sentence is usually the whole obstacle.
- Ask two colleagues who know a market you do not for three named introductions each, then tell the new contacts outright that you are new and trying to meet people. That is the whole of what Boniface did in Toronto.
- Give each room you work in one job for the next two weeks, and put a 30-minute walk in front of your first meeting to replace the commute you lost.
