## Why this episode

You have never decided what you want people to think of you, so the decision is being made on your behalf by your email signature, your voicemail greeting and the business card that has been folded in your wallet for a year. Kyle Lindenboom runs individuals through the same vision, diligence and gap-analysis process his agency runs for corporate brand clients, and he is blunt that a brand projecting forty attributes projects nothing. You should come out of it with three attributes and a list of places your behavior contradicts them.

## Guest

Kyle Lindenboom is founder and CEO of InTandem Marketing, which he started about three years before this conversation on the belief that a great brand is not reserved for the Apples, Googles and Amazons of the world. His firm built the Connection Builders and Branch Out brands, and he walks through the reasoning on air: why the logo is a tree, why the leaves are connected dots, why green, and why "excel" replaced "win" in the tagline. He began on the agency side in Detroit, dressed the part, and watched how people treated him change when he stopped. Find him at [linkedin.com/in/kyle-lindenboom-71a59916](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kyle-lindenboom-71a59916/).

## Key ideas

**A brand is not a logo; it is the crystallization of why you exist, who you serve and why they choose you over the alternative.** The logo, the website, the tagline and even the color palette are vehicles for telling that story. Lindenboom's point is that you cannot design the vehicles until the story is settled.

**Your personal brand is the sum of all perceptions other people hold about you, and everything you do or fail to do moves it.** Clothes, gait, haircut, whether you shaved, how the email opened. Lindenboom argues the mechanism is identical to the music and the smell inside a retail store.

**Awareness is the goal, not behavior change.** If you love BMWs, drive one. What Lindenboom rejects is buying a car or a persona to manufacture a perception you do not actually own, which produces the whiplash of bouncing between identities that were never yours.

**Run the corporate process on yourself: define the ideal perception, do diligence on the current state, then write the gap analysis.** He is careful that gaps are opportunities rather than failures, and insists on naming the audience first, because a brand without a target audience is not a brand.

**You cannot brand around forty attributes.** Lindenboom's phrase is that jack of all trades, master of none is very real in branding, and the strongest personal and corporate brands are narrow and unapologetically specific. Forced to choose between "high integrity" and "professional and buttoned up," you pick, and the picking is the point.

**A brand creates expectations, which makes inconsistency the actual damage.** He has worked with excellent account managers at both extremes, the always-on one and the one who stops at 5:01 on Friday. Neither is wrong. Being the first and then behaving like the second is what breaks trust.

## Try this

- Take thirty minutes this week and write down three brand attributes you want, each with a one-sentence definition of what it means to you. Then list where your current actions align and where they do not. That is the challenge Alex Drost sets at the end of the episode.
- Audit the artifacts nobody thinks about: email signature, voicemail greeting, how your last five emails opened, the condition of your business cards, your website. Fix the one that most plainly contradicts your three words.
- Decide your responsiveness rule, always-on or business hours, and say it out loud to a client or a colleague this week. Lindenboom's position is that either works and drifting between them does not.
