## Why this episode

You are stuck on something and have not named which kind of stuck it is. Lisa Howze splits it in two: a setback is an unforeseen event that halts your progress, and a disappointment is an expectation you had already tasted before it slipped away. They call for different responses, and treating one as the other is why people stall. She works through both from her own record — two evictions before she was nine, failing the CPA exam twice, and running a mayor's office in a city that was in bankruptcy.

## Guest

Lisa Howze is a certified public accountant and the author of Candy Girl Mentality, which was forthcoming at the time of this recording. She served as a Michigan state representative, ran for mayor of Detroit, and then served as Chief of Staff and chief government affairs officer to Detroit Mayor Mike Duggan, joining the administration in 2014 while the city was in bankruptcy. She later moved to a vice presidency at a private university in Detroit. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lisahowze/) · [Candy Girl Mentality](https://candygirlmentality.com/the-book/)

## Key ideas

**A setback and a major disappointment are not the same problem.** A setback is something you did not see coming — the market in the year you planned to retire, a hurricane, a death in the family. A disappointment is an unfulfilled expectation: the pitch you could taste, the deck you built, the client that went elsewhere. Howze's point is that resilience starts with knowing which one you are holding.

**Entrepreneurship is a lens rather than a job title.** She describes an entrepreneur's job as identifying and solving problems, and says she carried that posture from a childhood lemonade-and-popcorn stand into the statehouse and the mayor's office. She was also, at ten, checking the forecast to decide how much lemonade to make.

**It takes imagination more than capital.** A thirteen-dollar stake in M&M candies compounded into more than four thousand dollars in savings by high school graduation, and paid her first year at the University of Michigan. She uses it as evidence against the most common excuse she hears, which is not having enough to start with.

**When someone has already done what you keep failing at, take their plan and follow it literally.** Howze failed the CPA exam twice and swore off it. Then she asked a colleague who had passed what she actually did — study before work, at lunch, after work, nine hours a day on weekends — and her first reaction was that it was too much. She caught herself: you asked for the blueprint, so who are you not to follow it. On the third sitting she passed three parts.

**Feeling in over your head is the signal to stand up, not to retreat.** Her analogy is a wave that knocked her down at the beach while she screamed for help, until her friend told her to stand up — the water was shallow. Applied to a new leader, it means acting on the information you have and making the call rather than waiting for the complete picture.

**Imposter syndrome does not yield to affirmation.** Howze's method is specific: find the one thing inside your wheelhouse that you do better than anyone in the room, claim it publicly as your area of authority, and then deliver a result. She is explicit that this is not positive thinking. She pairs it with the observation that every promotion restarts you at a new level with a new set of written and unwritten rules, and that it is the unwritten ones that trip people.

## Try this

- Write one sentence naming whether the thing weighing on you is a setback or a disappointment. If it is a disappointment, write down the expectation you set, since that is the part you controlled.
- Find one person who has already passed the exam, closed the client, or made the jump you keep missing. Ask them what a normal week looked like, in hours, and then run their schedule without negotiating it down.
- Pick the one area of your current role you could defensibly call yourself the expert in. Volunteer for a piece of work in it in the next two weeks and put your name on the output.
