## Why this episode

You have a LinkedIn account you barely touch, and a quiet suspicion that you have nothing worth saying on it. George Khalife's answer is that you are trying to do the wrong thing: stop producing advice and start documenting what you actually went through, because your own experience is the one claim nobody can dispute. This is for professionals who have decided that the risk of being judged is a good enough reason to stay invisible.

## Guest

George Khalife works for the Toronto Stock Exchange out of Chicago, helping Midwest companies access the Canadian public markets. He hosts the podcast Let's Grab Coffee, which he started by Googling how to start a podcast, and which earned him "What are you? Jimmy Fallon?" from peers in capital markets before it connected him to founders and investors he could not have reached otherwise. He is on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/georgekhalife/).

## Key ideas

**LinkedIn is under-used by exactly the people best positioned to use it.** Khalife's rough math: hundreds of millions of accounts, active monthly users in the low millions, and a far smaller number posting anything original. The gap between having a profile and using the platform is the whole opportunity.

**Documenting your experience beats dispensing advice.** His first article was four years of lessons written the month he graduated, and what it taught him was the difference between telling people what to do and telling them what happened to you. One invites argument. The other cannot be argued with, because it is yours.

**Storytelling and idea generation are muscles, and reps are the only way to build them.** Khalife rejects the idea that either is innate. Publishing generates the feedback that tells you what resonates, which is why waiting for a polished first version is the expensive choice — he reaches for Reid Hoffman's line about launching too late.

**White space is where the work ideas come from, and it has to be scheduled like a meeting.** He used to treat empty calendar time as dead time. The "did you know" series he now runs every Friday came out of a jog, not a thirty-person meeting.

**Build relationships in person and use the platforms to keep them alive.** Khalife has lived across three regions and cannot maintain those relationships by phone. Online is the maintenance layer for a network that starts at events, meet-ups and introductions — not a replacement for them.

**Being early in your career is an asset in this, not a disqualification.** Young professionals tell him they have no standing to post without a VP or CFO title. He argues the opposite: showing up in public in your twenties is unusual enough that people give you their attention.

## Try this

- Write 300 words on the last project that did not go the way you planned — what you expected, what happened, what you would do differently. Publish it before you are proud of it.
- For the next two weeks, spend the first twenty minutes of your day reading, commenting on and sharing what your own contacts posted, before you post anything yourself.
- Pick five people who are known in the industry you want to be known in. Put their profiles next to yours, list what yours is missing, and fix two of those things this week.
