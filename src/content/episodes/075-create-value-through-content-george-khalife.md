## Why this episode

You have been told to build a personal brand and every honest look at the numbers tells you it is not working. George Khalife's argument is that the numbers were never the point: the return on publishing is the private one, the reps that make you better in the room when a real client conversation finally happens. This is for professionals in audit, law, banking or private equity who have been measuring the wrong thing and are close to quitting because of it.

## Guest

George Khalife is Vice President of US Capital Formation with the Toronto Stock Exchange, where he works with private companies in the Midwest looking to raise public capital in Canada. He also hosts Let's Grab Coffee, a Canadian business podcast he started around 2016 when he was 22 or 23 — a time when, as he puts it, doing this in capital markets was still nascent. This is his second appearance on Branch Out. He is on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/georgekhalife).

## Key ideas

**The return on creating content is private before it is public.** Khalife started the podcast for a selfish reason — an hour with a CEO he could not otherwise get. Alex Drost makes the parallel case from the host chair: the writing and recording are reps and mental rigor you do not encounter in normal day-to-day work, and they show up later in conversations that have nothing to do with the content.

**Downloads, views and impressions are the wrong scoreboard because you do not control them.** Khalife replaces them with micro-goals he can control — twenty quality, well-recorded episodes out by year end. He points out he did not know his show was ranked until someone emailed him about it.

**Plan on your first year being bad and publish it anyway.** Almost nobody crosses a million subscribers on one viral video; Khalife's suggested exercise is to go find any large YouTube channel and watch its oldest uploads. He cites Guy Kawasaki's version of the same advice: the best entrepreneurs just chip.

**Nerves do not go away, and the goal is a middle amount of them, not zero.** Khalife has interviewed the co-founders of Netflix and Starbucks and still feels it every time. Too little nervousness reads as egotistical or uninterested; too much reads as not knowing your subject.

**Reps are half the practice — the other half is studying yourself on tape.** Athletes and fighters review footage and business people mostly do not. Khalife found he was blinking constantly on camera and interjecting "yup" over guests; Drost found he had said "let's unpack that" roughly 25 times in a single episode.

**Energy is an output of inputs you choose, not a mood that happens to you.** Sleep, exercise, what you eat before an event, what you scroll before you walk in. Khalife's rule for platforms follows the same logic: pick the one or two you actually resonate with, and understand you only get out of a platform what you put into it.

## Try this

- Write down one output goal for the next twelve months that is entirely inside your control — twelve posts, twenty recordings, one talk a quarter — and delete downloads, views and follower count from anything you track.
- Record your next internal presentation or client pitch and watch it back once, all the way through. Note two habits you did not know you had, then stop watching. Khalife's fiancée caught his blinking; you will not catch yours from the inside.
- Take one question clients ask you every month and write the answer this week as a short post, explained the way you would explain it to a five-year-old. Publish it even if it is not good.
