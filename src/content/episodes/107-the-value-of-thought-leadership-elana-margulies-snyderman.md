## Why this episode

Your firm wants content and nobody can tell you what it is supposed to return. Elana Margulies Snyderman runs thought leadership for a global audit and tax firm, and her test is narrow: if a reader or listener could get the information somewhere else, it does not qualify. Take this as a working account of what firm content is actually for — being recalled when the buyer finally needs you — and of why a blog on LinkedIn is a better first move than a podcast.

## Guest

Elana Margulies Snyderman is a director at EisnerAmper, where she manages thought leadership for the firm. She came to it from financial journalism: Institutional Investor's Alternative Investment News, then eight years building US hedge fund coverage for HFMWeek, where she also moderated a monthly breakfast panel series and hosted dinners for hedge fund COOs. She hosts two podcast series — one on financial services, one on private equity deal-making — and writes a weekly financial services blog. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/elanamarguliessnyderman/)

## Key ideas

**Thought leadership is defined by the information, not the format.** Her definition is intelligence a reader or listener could not find elsewhere, delivered through an article, blog, podcast or video — the medium is a distribution choice. Content that repackages what is already available is something else, whatever you call it internally.

**The return is recall, not leads.** The point of firm content is that the buyer keeps you top of mind for the moment they need audit, tax or advisory work. Direct inquiries do happen — she can point to one podcast that produced them — but treating them as the metric will get the program cancelled.

**Point the content at the guest, not at the firm.** Margulies Snyderman keeps performance figures and fund names out because compliance requires it and because a podcast is not a sales piece. The payoff runs through the guest: an allocator searching for a woman-owned private equity firm found one of her episodes and approached that fund about a co-investment.

**Start where production cost is lowest.** Her recommendation for anyone building a presence is a blog distributed on LinkedIn, where reach already exists. Alex Drost, who went the other way in April 2020, does the arithmetic out loud: writing 120 blog posts is a great deal easier than producing 120 episodes, and the writing itself develops the thinking you later need on air.

**Regulated guests need the questions in advance.** Private fund managers often cannot discuss performance and sometimes cannot name their funds, so she sends talking points ahead of recording. Drost pushes back — he prefers unscripted conversation for the tangents it produces — which is worth hearing as a genuine trade-off rather than a settled rule.

## Try this

- Name the one thing you know that a client could not find through a search, and turn it into a short post on LinkedIn this week. If you cannot name it, what you are planning is a brochure.
- Volunteer to moderate one panel at a conference you already attend. Open with two show-of-hands polling questions and take audience questions throughout rather than saving them for the final five minutes.
- Ask every new contact this month to introduce you to two or three people they respect in the business — the question Margulies Snyderman used to build a hedge fund network from nothing.
