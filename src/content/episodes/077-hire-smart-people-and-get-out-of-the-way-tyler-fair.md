## Why this episode

You are the bottleneck in your own team and you know it, because every process still routes through you. Tyler Fair bootstrapped SourceScrub from a manual conference-list scrub into a company of roughly 65 domestic staff and about 750 internationally, and his method for scaling was to keep removing jobs from his own plate. If you are deciding what to look for in the next hire and how much of the work to specify, this is a founder describing what he actually did.

## Guest

Tyler Fair is co-founder and CEO of SourceScrub, a data service for dealmakers researching founder-owned businesses. He spent about seven years in direct origination, including at Serent Capital in San Francisco, where the private company data he needed did not exist in usable form. He left after a six-month transition, with Serent as customer number one, and built the company without venture capital until a 2019 recapitalization with Mainsail. Find him at [linkedin.com/in/tyler-fair-63a3657](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tyler-fair-63a3657/).

## Key ideas

**The product came from being the customer for three and a half years too long.** Fair hired interns to scrub Google for conference exhibitor, sponsor and speaker lists, recruited three cohorts a year, and watched them quit early. He built the thing he wanted to use, validated it by doing the work by hand and taking the output to peers, and made his own employer the first buyer.

**Fair screens candidates on what they ask about first.** When someone opens an interview asking about benefits, he tunes out. He is looking for people who want the experience of learning how a business gets built, and he tells them the reward is a story they own for the rest of their career, not a package.

**A survived hold period beats a failed startup on a resume.** Fair's argument for joining a bootstrapped company is that nobody values two years at a venture-backed startup that went bust, which he puts at north of 90% of the time. What holds value is being able to say you were there through the hold period and the exit, and what you did to the business in between.

**The answer to a scaling problem is never more of the founder's time.** Fair describes systematically firing himself from jobs: find something that works, hire someone with a process-building, figure-it-out mentality, stay out of the sausage making, then iterate on what they produce. He notes it is also the easier way to manage.

**Offshore teams are an investment in people, not a cost line.** SourceScrub's data operations sit in the Philippines and Ukraine. Recorded weeks into the invasion, Fair talks about the Lviv team and about the domestic staff running a fundraiser for Philippine colleagues after the December typhoon, and treats that response as the return on trusting people with opportunity.

**Ask customers what is not working and let them set the roadmap.** Fair says his favourite inbound message is a complaint. SourceScrub runs a customer advisory panel of leading private equity firms and investment banks, and builds at their direction.

## Try this

- List the five recurring tasks that still route through you and pick the one you are least essential to. Name the person who takes it over and hand it to them this month with a goal line rather than a process.
- Change one question in your next interview: instead of describing the benefits, ask the candidate what they want to be able to say about this job in five years, and listen for whether the answer is an experience or a package.
- Email three customers this week asking only what is not working. Do not ask how you are doing.
