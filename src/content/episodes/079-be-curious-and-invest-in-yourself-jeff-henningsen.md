## Why this episode

You have been meaning to start the thing — the podcast, the newsletter, the practice group — and you are still planning it. Jeff Henningsen mentors young professionals who do exactly this, and his case is that the planning is the procrastination. The second half is for anyone who has been told their introversion is a business development liability.

## Guest

Jeff Henningsen is President of Lockton Companies in Texas, focused on M&A insurance services, and has spent 26 years serving the private equity world. He hosts Private Equity Fast Pitch and is known in the industry as the podfather of private equity; he edited the first 60 episodes himself. Before insurance, he built and sold a lawn mowing business at 22 and spent four years working for a Dallas private equity investor. He is on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jeff-henningsen-181b70a/).

## Key ideas

**Ideas are the cheap part, and months of planning is procrastination wearing a suit.** Henningsen mentors young people who spend months planning execution instead of starting. His preference is to take the risk early and then kill or tweak the idea fast, because the failure is where the learning is.

**Coaching is what people at the top of a field do continuously, so treating it as remedial is backwards.** He describes the range at a pro tournament: the top 150 golfers in the world with two or three coaches each, and not just for the swing — mental, diet, lifting. His blunt corollary is that a lot of what gets called psychology is coaching that a stigmatized word keeps people from seeking.

**Accountability, not instruction, is the coach's main product.** Most people cannot hold themselves accountable alone, which is why the workout buddy works. Henningsen also adds a reframe worth keeping: nobody coaches you unless they think you have potential.

**Content is relationship infrastructure, and the right first question is what your why is — not what gear to buy.** Henningsen ran a Houston and Dallas happy hour for 19 years, wanted out by year 16, and needed something to replace it that would keep 2,500 email addresses warm without pitching insurance. The podcast was the replacement. Total startup spend: about $100, against friends who rented or built studios.

**Introversion is an asset in relationship-driven business, not a handicap to be managed.** Henningsen learned early to become a very good asker of questions, and he lets silence do work most extroverts interrupt. His framing is the orchestra conductor: back to the audience, pointing at the people who should be heard.

**Introvert and extrovert describe what depletes you, not what you can perform.** He can address a thousand people from a stage, and it exhausts him. He argues you can also be coached to act as your opposite when the situation demands it, as long as you budget for the recovery.

## Try this

- Take the thing you have been planning for more than a month and ship the cheapest possible version of it within two weeks. Recruit three friends as guinea pigs the way Henningsen did rather than waiting for the right first guest.
- Ask one person to be your accountability check on a single commitment, tell them the specific thing and the date, and put the check-in on both calendars. Henningsen's point is that this is the part a coach actually sells you.
- In your next three client or internal meetings, ask a question and then count to three before you say anything after the answer. Henningsen's rule: let silence work for you, and see what the other person adds.
