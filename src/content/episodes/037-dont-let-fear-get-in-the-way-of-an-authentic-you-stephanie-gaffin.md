## Why this episode

You know roughly who you are and you still spend most of your working week performing a slightly different person. Stephanie Gaffin has a specific diagnosis for that gap, and it is not your firm or your calendar: it is fear, and mostly fear of something that has not happened. If you have ever drafted a LinkedIn comment, reread it four times and closed the tab, this hour is about what that costs you.

## Guest

Stephanie Gaffin is Managing Member and COO of SLS Capital Advisors, a lower middle market investment banking firm. She cohosts Right in the Middle Market, a podcast on running, growing and selling a middle market business. She talks openly here about a cancer diagnosis and a long diagnostic process, and uses it as her working example of choosing a response to an event she did not choose. Find her on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stephaniegaffin/).

## Key ideas

**Two facades cannot connect, which makes authenticity a precondition for relationships rather than a nice quality to have.** Gaffin's starting point is Brené Brown's claim that humans are wired for connection. Her addition is the mechanical consequence: if the thing showing up is a constructed version of you, whatever forms is not a relationship with you.

**Authenticity is a decision you make situation by situation, not a fixed self you go looking for.** Gaffin rejects the etched-in-stone version outright. The real question is how you want to show up in the specific thing coming next, and the answer can be anger, humor, compassion or stoicism depending on who you are becoming.

**Fear and anxiety are different problems, and only one of them is worth acting on.** Fear concerns a threat in front of you right now, which you can deal with. Anxiety projects onto a future that may never arrive. Gaffin picked the distinction up from a Minecraft novel she was reading with her nine-year-old, and it is the sharpest tool in the episode.

**Not posting is not the safe option; it is a way of not showing up.** You have an interesting thought, you cannot word it in the twenty minutes you have, so you skip it. Gaffin's point is that the skipped comment is not neutral. Engagement is one of the ways connection happens now, and a filter set too strong precludes it entirely.

**A one-minute floor is enough, and setting a heroic target is how people quit.** Gaffin promised herself one minute of meditation a night with a goal of ten, and says the shift came from the one-minute version. She does nothing elaborate: she sits and focuses on the breath, and practices noticing that a thought arriving in your head does not make it true or yours.

**Event plus reaction equals outcome is how you get agency back in a situation you did not pick.** Gaffin borrows the frame from Jack Canfield. She could not choose the cancer or the MRIs. She could order a sticker that read "One more MRI, and I'll stick to the fridge," wear it into the scan, and start feeling like herself again.

## Try this

- Pick one specific thing on this week's calendar — a client meeting, dinner with your spouse — and decide in advance how you want to show up. Do it, then write two paragraphs that night on whether you did. This is Gaffin's own call to action and it takes about ten minutes total.
- Set a one-minute meditation floor tonight, before bed, and hold it for two weeks before you increase it. If you want structure, Gaffin used Calm's 7 Days of Calm and 21 Days of Calm; Alex uses Headspace.
- Post the comment you talked yourself out of last week. Five minutes of brain-dump journaling first if it helps — start with "I don't know what I want to write about today" and keep the pen moving.
