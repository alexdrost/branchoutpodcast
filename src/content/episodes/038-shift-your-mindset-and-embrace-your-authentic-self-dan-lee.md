## Why this episode

You have been told business development is sales, and you have been treating every call as an audit of whether you got something out of it. Dan Lee ran that way for a decade inside a private equity firm and nearly lost his seat. This is what changed when he stopped keeping score that way, and what it cost him to find out.

## Guest

Dan Lee is a partner with Comvest Partners, a multi-billion-dollar private investment firm providing equity and debt capital to middle-market companies. He moved into business development after his partners told him he was not suited to running the firm's administration, and he says he treated the move as a demotion for years. He is unusually direct in this conversation about the annual review, the divorce, and the junior colleagues who were lapping him. Find him on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dan-lee-748b92b/).

## Key ideas

**Business development stops working the moment you file it under sales.** Lee spent years walking into calls asking what he could extract, and he says the people on the other side could tell. The reframe that moved his numbers was building a community and treating an actual deal as a rare intersection of two lines that seldom cross.

**The calls that land are the ones you make when there is nothing to pitch.** In the spring of 2020 his restaurant and consumer deals looked finished and he assumed he might be out of a job, so he started hammering the phones with no agenda. People were glad to hear from him precisely because nobody else was calling.

**Managing how your career looks is a slower, more expensive failure than a bad year.** Lee says he spent a huge chunk of his career worried about what he looked like rather than what he was, and he extends the same diagnosis to a marriage he wanted to look good more than to be good.

**Being good at several things beats being great at one.** Lee traded Muay Thai, which he took up to show people he could kick ass, for yoga, and reorganized around balance across work, fitness, and being a father. He frames his old all-or-nothing training swings as image management wearing gym clothes.

**Consuming self-improvement content is a way of avoiding the work.** After his divorce he stacked meditation, podcasts, and books looking for the answer, then noticed he had put in about an hour of actual work by the end of the day. His conclusion is that implementation is the only variable that ever moved.

## Try this

- Block ninety minutes this week for calls to people you have nothing to sell right now, and open each one with what you could learn from them rather than what you need.
- Pick one discipline with no visible daily progress and nothing impressive to show at the end of it, and do it every day for a month. Lee's are the splits, pull-ups, and lap swimming.
- Take the next seven days to write down the specific daily actions that would move you toward the most authentic version of yourself, then put them somewhere you will see them tomorrow.
