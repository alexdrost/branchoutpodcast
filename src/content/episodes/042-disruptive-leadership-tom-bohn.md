## Why this episode

You are trying to move an organization that has a very firm idea of what it is, and the resistance is arriving as reasonable-sounding requests for more information. Tom Bohn took over the Association for Corporate Growth weeks before the pandemic removed the live events its members thought it existed to run, and had to argue in his own job interview that the organization was describing itself wrong. This is what that argument sounds like, including the parts about consolidating 59 chapters and the diversity plan he refused to publish unfunded.

## Guest

Tom Bohn is President and CEO of the Association for Corporate Growth, the global community for middle market M&A dealmakers. Before ACG he led a veterinary media group he says went from 11 to 26 million in revenue in five years and became the dominant media player in its industry inside eighteen months. He sits on ACG's diversity work with board chair Marty Okner. Find him on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tombohn/).

## Key ideas

**The disruptive act is redefining what business you are in, and everything else is machinery for surviving that redefinition.** Bohn told his hiring committee they were an event organization only if they chose to be, and that ACG is a connecting and media company serving middle market dealmakers. Live events are one modality inside that. Refusing the reframe, he argues, is what would have killed the organization when the Hiltons closed.

**"We need more detail" is almost always disagreement wearing a costume.** Bohn separates the two objections cleanly: not believing in the path forward is a fundamentally different problem from lacking information, and only one of them is solved by another deck. He names fear of failure and fear of not having all the answers as the two excuses that keep organizations from noticing they have stopped growing.

**Disruptive change needs three cultural conditions, and none of them is a strategy document.** An abundance of transparency, to the point where nobody who claims not to know the direction was actually listening. Honesty that takes emotion out of performance conversations, including your own. And personal connection that reaches people's anxiety about their families, because you are moving their cheese daily.

**Roll change out as a framework and let the affected groups build the operating detail with you.** When One ACG launched it was deliberately incomplete. Bohn's answer to "we need more detail" was to build the detail together, and he credits the ownership that produced as the difference between success and failure.

**Consensus is the goal, unanimity is the standard, and conflict is the mechanism rather than the failure.** A 50-plus-1 vote tells Bohn he did not bring the stakeholders along. He wants the tough conversation, is willing to say he disagrees outright, and draws his line at the point where disagreement becomes personal. Alex adds Lencioni's read that "we'll agree to disagree" is a refusal to engage.

**A diversity plan that is not funded and not measurable becomes a document nobody acts on.** Bohn's trigger was eight ACG chapters issuing eight contradictory statements in 2020. His answer was a coordinated commitment through parity.org — at least one candidate of the opposite sex and one person of color seriously considered for every senior opening — plus dashboards that track rather than judge. When someone asked how white male executives could lead this, his answer was that it does not disqualify you, it means you will not see every angle.

## Try this

- Write one sentence naming the business your team is actually in, without using the name of any product or event you currently run. Then check last quarter's budget against it and find the line that contradicts you.
- Take the next objection you hear that starts "we need more detail" and ask directly whether the person believes the direction is right. Handle the answer you get, not the one they gave you.
- For your next senior-level opening, commit in writing that at least one candidate of the opposite sex and one person of color will be seriously considered, and attach a number and an owner to it before the role is posted.
