## Why this episode

Busy season takes sixty-plus hours a week and every year you tell yourself the next one will be different. Dan Montgomery's firm caps everyone at 50 hours even in season, and the mechanism is not stamina — it is refusing certain clients and publishing a firm-level week that individuals can build around. If you have been told the math cannot work, here is a partner showing his.

## Guest

Dan Montgomery is an audit partner and director of business development at Forge Financial and Management Consulting, and a CPA. He spent about nine years at TDT CPA Advisors; days before this recording, the audit and business intelligence practices split off from TDT to form Forge, and he is still catching himself saying the old name. He grew up in Alaska and now lives in Iowa. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dan-montgomery-68519837/)

## Key ideas

**Capping hours increased output rather than reducing it.** The year Forge adopted the Full Focus system, Montgomery says the firm worked fewer total hours than the year before and billed more charged hours. Personally, he counts roughly three weeks over 45 hours across the whole year as a CPA firm partner.

**The 50-hour cap is a client selection decision, not a willpower decision.** Forge takes on no new tax-only clients, because year-round engagements spread work across the calendar instead of compressing it into one season — and because accepting work the firm cannot staff inside the cap is, in his framing, failing the staff.

**If anybody could do the work, you are not adding value — and that work is what burns people out.** Montgomery connects client selection directly to employee happiness and retention: people who come in every day on engagements they see no value in will go find something else.

**Three priorities a day and three a week; the constraint is the point.** He keeps the weekly big three on a sticky note on his monitor and sets tomorrow's three at the end of today, so he is not thinking about work at home. Focus on more than three and you jump between them and finish none.

**A running task list lets you dodge the one item that matters.** You skip the dreaded task in favor of quick wins you enjoy. His counter is two hours first thing every Friday morning to clear the list so nothing rolls into Monday to haunt him again.

**Business development is not sales, and because it drains you, batch it.** Montgomery says flatly that he is not a salesman; reframing BD as connecting and helping is what turned it from terrifying into something he loves. He now stacks those meetings on designated days rather than dropping them wherever they fit, because switching between review work and BD costs disproportionate energy.

## Try this

- Go through last season's client list and mark every engagement you touch only once a year. Pick the category you will stop accepting new versions of, and decide now who you will refer those inquiries to.
- Put three items for this week on a sticky note on your monitor, and before you close the laptop tonight write down tomorrow's three. Block 8:00 to 10:00 on Friday to clear whatever is left over.
- Take next month's coffees, lunches and intro calls off the calendar and re-book them onto two days, then hold the mornings on the other three for review work.
